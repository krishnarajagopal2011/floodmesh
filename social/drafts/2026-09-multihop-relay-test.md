# Multi-hop test: one relay carries an alarm to 550 m

Tags: #trials #feedback
Channels: X, Instagram reel
Visual: real clips from the 26 September test: the sender unit's screen
sending the alarm, the relay unit being held at road level, and the
responder unit's screen when it arrives. A simple map graphic with the
three points and distances marked is fine alongside real footage, not
instead of it.

## Facts

Internal only. The post text names no radio settings.

- Test on 26 September 2026, firmware_v3 3.1.0. Three units: sender,
  relay, responder. docs/field-tests.md, "2026-09-26: SOS through one
  relay".
- Straight from the sender to the responder, the alarm reached 350 m, then
  nothing past that. docs/field-tests.md, Results table, "A → E direct".
- With one relay in between: sender to relay, 300 m, line of sight,
  received; relay to responder, 250 m, line of sight, received, failed
  beyond 250 m. Total reach through the relay: 550 m. docs/field-tests.md,
  Results table.
- The relay stayed at road level the whole 15-minute test; it was never
  raised up. docs/field-tests.md, "Relay height".
- It only worked where the two units could see each other; standing in
  front of a shop worked better than sitting inside it, and holding the
  unit out of a car window worked better than inside with the windows up.
  docs/field-tests.md, "Other observations".
- Not measured: signal strength at every point, what exactly stood between
  the relay and the responder where the link failed, the relay at any
  greater height, and how much battery the test used.
  docs/field-tests.md, "Not measured".
- Next planned test: raise the relay onto a terrace, balcony or a pole of
  6 m or more, then re-test. docs/field-tests.md, "Next test".

## Instagram caption

#trials #feedback

We are building an off-grid pager for floods. First relay test: one extra
unit passing a message on carried it further than sending it straight.

Straight from the sender, the alarm reached about 350 m, the same as our
first test. Adding one relay in between carried it to about 550 m total:
300 m to the relay, then 250 m more to the person who could help. The
relay stayed at road level the whole time, it was never raised up, and it
only worked where the units could see each other.

We did not record signal strength at every point, so we cannot yet say
exactly why the last hop stopped where it did. Next test: raise the relay
onto a terrace or balcony and see how much further that reaches. Every
result goes out, good or bad.

If you were placing one relay on your street to help messages travel
further, where would you put it: a rooftop, a balcony, or somewhere else?

Why: in Chennai 2015 and during Cyclone Michaung, towers and power failed
together. Neighbours 200 m apart could not reach each other.
FloodMesh passes SOS alerts and short text messages between buildings with
no tower and no internet. It is designed to run for days on one charge.
Built in public.
